GreenRoute

A route planner for delivery fleets that counts load, CO₂ and cost, not just distance. I started it because I was worried about the emissions from 10-minute delivery apps. Read the case study.

What the evidence shows

  • Within 0.2% of the proven optimum on A-n32-k5, and 1.1% on M-n101-k10.
  • Beat Google OR-Tools at the time limits tested on both.
  • Rewrote an encoding that behaved like random search.
  • Over 300 automated tests.

Where it falls short

  • Loses to Clarke-Wright and OR-Tools on X-n101-k25.
  • Emission factors are modelled assumptions, not measurements.
  • Road routing has not been tested against a live router.
  • Tested on benchmark and simulated data, not real fleets.

GreenRoute: fixing a solver that behaved like random search

Case study. The first version of my route optimiser scored about 100% above the best known answer. This is how I found out why, and what I changed.

The problem

Small delivery fleets plan routes by distance, but a heavy parcel carried across town burns more fuel than a light one. I wanted a planner that counts load, CO₂ and cost together, and I wanted to build the optimiser myself: a Quantum-behaved particle swarm (QPSO).

What I tried first

Each particle held one number per customer, and each number was turned into a vehicle with a modulo. On the standard A-n32-k5 benchmark, that version ended up far from the optimum.

Why it failed

A change of 0.001 in one number could send that customer to a different vehicle. Every move of the swarm scrambled the whole plan, so it was effectively random search. I wrote a regression test that pins this behaviour down.

What I changed

I replaced it with a cluster-first, route-second design. A particle is now just the centres of the vehicles. Customers go to the nearest centre that still has capacity, heaviest first, and each route is ordered with nearest-neighbour plus 2-opt. Small moves now change only a few customers on the boundaries, so progress is smooth. I added local search on top and kept the old encoding only for comparison.

Results

All algorithms get the same budget of evaluations and are scored by the same evaluator. A-n32-k5 has a proven optimum of 784.

Algorithm (A-n32-k5, 8 seeds)DistanceGap to optimum
Cluster QPSO + local search785.60.2%
OR-Tools (5 s)796.01.5%
Clarke-Wright savings839.07.0%
Original random-key QPSO1618.4106.4%

On M-n101-k10 (optimum 820), cluster QPSO with local search reached 828.7, which is 1.1% above the optimum. That was ahead of Clarke-Wright (833) and OR-Tools at 10 s (845).

What it still gets wrong

On X-n101-k25, with many tight vehicles, Clarke-Wright and OR-Tools both beat it. It is slower: about 30 seconds on the 100-customer instance against 10 for OR-Tools. Three instances do not prove it is better in general, and OR-Tools was given short time limits. Emission factors are modelled assumptions, and road routing has not been tested against a live router.

What I would do next

Test on real delivery data, try more instances, and speed up route ordering inside each evaluation.
